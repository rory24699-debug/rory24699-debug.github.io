📘 Grade 4 Mathematics Notes

Building Strong Foundations in Numeracy

Read first, then watch, then practise.

1. Numbers to 10,000

Place Value: Every digit has a value depending on its position.

Thousands – Hundreds – Tens – Ones.

Example 1: 5,372 = 5,000 + 300 + 70 + 2
Example 2: In 7,481, the 7 represents 7,000.
Example 3: In 3,625, the 6 represents 600.
Example 4: Write 4,209 in words: Four thousand, two hundred and nine.
Remember: The position of a digit determines its value.

2. Addition and Subtraction

Addition combines numbers. Subtraction finds the difference or what remains.

Line up digits according to their place value. Start from the Ones column.

Example 1: 2,456 + 1,329 = 3,785
Example 2: 3,675 + 2,148 = 5,823
Example 3: 5,864 − 2,321 = 3,543
Example 4: 7,000 − 2,456 = 4,544
Remember: Check subtraction by adding your answer to the number taken away.

3. Multiplication

Multiplication means adding equal groups together.

For example, 3 × 4 means 3 groups of 4.

Example 1: 3 × 4 = 12
Example 2: 6 × 5 = 30
Example 3: 24 × 3 = 72
20 × 3 = 60; 4 × 3 = 12; 60 + 12 = 72.
Example 4: 32 × 4 = 128
30 × 4 = 120; 2 × 4 = 8; 120 + 8 = 128.
Remember: Practise your multiplication tables to improve speed and accuracy.

4. Division

Division means sharing equally or finding how many groups can be made.

Example 1: 12 ÷ 3 = 4
Example 2: 20 ÷ 5 = 4
Example 3: 36 ÷ 6 = 6
Example 4: 48 ÷ 4 = 12
Remember: Check division by multiplying your answer by the divisor. For example, 12 × 4 = 48.

5. Fractions

A fraction represents part of a whole.

Numerator: The top number.
Denominator: The bottom number.

Example 1: 1/2 means one out of two equal parts.
Example 2: 1/4 means one out of four equal parts.
Example 3: 1/2 of 10 = 5.
Example 4: 1/4 of 20 = 5.
Remember: The denominator tells you how many equal parts make the whole.

6. Geometry

Geometry helps us understand shapes, lines and space.

Example 1: A square has 4 equal sides and 4 corners.
Example 2: A rectangle has 4 sides and 4 right angles. Opposite sides are equal.
Example 3: A triangle has 3 sides and 3 corners.
Example 4: A circle is round and has no straight sides or corners.
Remember: Count the sides and corners to help identify a shape.

7. Measurement and Money

Measurement helps us find length, mass, capacity and time. Money is used to calculate costs and change.

Example 1: 1 metre = 100 centimetres.
Example 2: 1 kilogram = 1,000 grams.
Example 3: $5.50 + $2.25 = $7.75.
Example 4: If you pay $100 for an item costing $65, your change is $35.
Remember: Always check the units when measuring and line up decimal points when adding money.

8. Data and Graphs

Data is information collected and organised. Graphs help us understand and compare information.

Example 1: A bar graph uses bars to compare different amounts.
Example 2: If 8 students like football and 5 like cricket, football is more popular.
Example 3: If a bar represents 10 books and another represents 15 books, the difference is 5 books.
Example 4: If Monday has 6 visitors and Tuesday has 9 visitors, the total is 15 visitors.
Remember: Read the title, labels and scale before answering questions about a graph.
