GRADE 5 – ABILITY DEVELOPMENT

Thinking Skills • Reasoning • Problem Solving • Study Skills

1. Critical Thinking

Critical thinking means carefully examining information before deciding what to believe or do.

How to Think Critically

  • Ask questions about information.
  • Look for facts and evidence.
  • Compare different ideas.
  • Check whether an answer makes sense.
  • Explain why you chose an answer.
Example:
A student says that all birds can fly. Is this correct?
Answer: No. Penguins and ostriches are birds, but they cannot fly.

2. Problem Solving

Problem solving involves understanding a challenge, choosing a suitable method and checking the answer.

Four Steps

  1. Understand the problem.
  2. Identify the important information.
  3. Choose a strategy and solve it.
  4. Check your answer.
Worked Example:
A school has 6 classes. Each class has 25 students. How many students are there altogether?

25 × 6 = 150 students.

3. Verbal Reasoning

Verbal reasoning uses words, language and relationships between words to solve problems.

Common Skills

  • Recognising word relationships.
  • Finding synonyms and antonyms.
  • Completing word patterns.
  • Understanding instructions.
  • Identifying words that do not belong.
Example – Analogy:
Bird is to nest as bee is to ______.
Answer: Hive.

4. Quantitative Reasoning

Quantitative reasoning means using numbers, patterns and mathematical relationships to solve problems.

Number Patterns

2, 4, 6, 8, ___
Answer: 10. Add 2 each time.
5, 10, 20, 40, ___
Answer: 80. Multiply by 2 each time.

5. Reading Comprehension Skills

Reading comprehension means understanding what you read and using information from a passage to answer questions.

  • Main idea – the central message.
  • Supporting details – information that explains the main idea.
  • Inference – using clues to work out an unstated idea.
  • Sequencing – arranging events in order.
  • Prediction – deciding what may happen next using clues.
Example:
"Janelle carried an umbrella and wore her raincoat. Dark clouds covered the sky."

Question: What might happen?
Answer: It may rain.

6. Memory and Concentration

Memory helps you remember information. Concentration helps you focus on a task.

Ways to Improve Memory

  • Review information regularly.
  • Use short notes and diagrams.
  • Repeat important facts aloud.
  • Connect new ideas to things you already know.
  • Get enough sleep and take short breaks.
Study Tip: Study one topic at a time. After reading, close your book and try to explain what you remember.

7. Study and Examination Skills

Good study habits help you prepare, manage your time and answer questions carefully.

Before an Examination

  • Follow a regular study timetable.
  • Review notes and examples.
  • Practise different types of questions.
  • Prepare materials the night before.

During an Examination

  • Read every question carefully.
  • Pay attention to instruction words.
  • Manage your time.
  • Check your answers when finished.

8. Following Instructions

Carefully following instructions helps you complete tasks correctly.

  • Read the full instruction before starting.
  • Look for words such as choose, explain, compare and identify.
  • Complete each step in the correct order.
  • Check that you have answered every part.
Example:
Instruction: Name two fruits and explain why they are healthy.
A complete answer must name two fruits AND give a reason they are healthy.

9. Patterns and Logical Thinking

Patterns follow a rule. Logical thinking helps you identify the rule and decide what comes next.

Example 1:
3, 6, 9, 12, ___
Answer: 15. Add 3.
Example 2:
A, C, E, G, ___
Answer: I. Skip one letter each time.

10. Making Good Decisions

Decision-making means choosing the best action after considering the available information.

  1. Identify the choice you need to make.
  2. Consider the possible options.
  3. Think about the results of each option.
  4. Choose the most suitable option.
  5. Review your decision.
