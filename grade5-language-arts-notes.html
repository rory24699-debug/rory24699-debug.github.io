GRADE 5 – LANGUAGE ARTS

Literacy • Curriculum Notes & Learning Examples

1. Parts of Speech

Parts of speech are groups of words that perform different jobs in sentences.

  • Noun: Names a person, place, animal or thing.
  • Pronoun: Replaces a noun.
  • Verb: Shows an action or state of being.
  • Adjective: Describes a noun.
  • Adverb: Describes a verb, adjective or another adverb.
  • Preposition: Shows the relationship between words.
  • Conjunction: Joins words or groups of words.
Example: The clever girl quickly completed her homework.
Noun: girl
Adjective: clever
Verb: completed
Adverb: quickly

2. Nouns

A noun names a person, place, animal, object or idea.

Types of Nouns

  • Common nouns – teacher, school, river.
  • Proper nouns – Jamaica, Kingston, Monday.
  • Collective nouns – team, flock, family.
  • Abstract nouns – honesty, courage, happiness.
Remember: Proper nouns begin with capital letters.

3. Verbs and Tenses

A verb tells what someone or something does, or describes a state of being.

Three Main Tenses

  • Present – She walks to school.
  • Past – She walked to school.
  • Future – She will walk to school.
Worked Example
Change this sentence to the past tense:
"John plays football."
Answer: "John played football."

4. Subject-Verb Agreement

The subject and verb in a sentence must agree in number.

Correct: The boy runs every morning.
Correct: The boys run every morning.
Incorrect: The boy run every morning.

5. Sentence Types

  • Declarative – makes a statement.
  • Interrogative – asks a question.
  • Imperative – gives a command or instruction.
  • Exclamatory – expresses strong feeling.
Statement: The children are reading.
Question: Are the children reading?
Command: Please read your book.
Exclamation: What an exciting story!

6. Punctuation and Capitalisation

Punctuation marks help readers understand written sentences.

  • Full stop (.) – ends a statement.
  • Comma (,) – separates items or parts of a sentence.
  • Question mark (?) – ends a question.
  • Exclamation mark (!) – shows strong emotion.
  • Apostrophe (') – shows possession or missing letters.
  • Quotation marks (" ") – show spoken words.
Correct sentence: "Where are you going?" asked Maria.

7. Reading Comprehension

Reading comprehension means understanding and interpreting what you read.

Important Reading Skills

  • Main idea – what the passage is mostly about.
  • Supporting details – facts that explain the main idea.
  • Inference – using clues to work out something not directly stated.
  • Vocabulary in context – understanding a word from the surrounding sentence.
  • Sequencing – putting events in the correct order.
Example:
"Dark clouds covered the sky. People hurried indoors and closed their windows."

Question: What can you infer?
Answer: Rain or a storm may be approaching.

8. Vocabulary Development

Vocabulary development helps students understand and use a wider range of words.

  • Synonyms – words with similar meanings.
  • Antonyms – words with opposite meanings.
  • Homophones – words that sound alike but have different meanings or spellings.
  • Prefixes – word parts added at the beginning.
  • Suffixes – word parts added at the end.
Happy – joyful (synonyms)
Hot – cold (antonyms)
Their – there (homophones)
Un + happy = unhappy (prefix)

9. Paragraph Writing

A paragraph is a group of sentences about one main idea.

A Good Paragraph Includes

  • Topic sentence – introduces the main idea.
  • Supporting sentences – give details and examples.
  • Concluding sentence – brings the paragraph to a close.
Example Topic: My School
My school is a wonderful place to learn. The teachers are helpful and encourage us to do our best. I enjoy reading in the library and playing with my friends during break. I am proud of my school.

10. Writing Skills

Good writing communicates ideas clearly and in an organized way.

  • Plan your ideas before writing.
  • Use complete sentences.
  • Organize ideas into paragraphs.
  • Use correct spelling and punctuation.
  • Check your work and make improvements.

11. Comprehension Practice Passage

Passage: A Visit to the Market

On Saturday morning, Keisha accompanied her mother to the market. They bought fresh mangoes, ripe bananas, carrots and tomatoes. Keisha helped to carry the shopping bags. Before leaving, her mother bought a coconut jelly for her as a reward.

Questions

  1. When did Keisha visit the market?
  2. Who accompanied Keisha?
  3. Name two fruits they bought.
  4. How did Keisha help her mother?
  5. Why do you think her mother bought her a coconut jelly?
Learning Tip: Read the passage twice. Look for evidence in the passage before answering.
