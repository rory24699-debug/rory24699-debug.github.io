MATHEMATICS PERFORMANCE TASK

Real-Life Problems, Mathematical Reasoning & Applications

Read each scenario carefully. Show your working, explain your thinking and check your answers.

ACTIVITY 1

PLANNING A SHOPPING BUDGET

Scenario: You are given $1,000 to purchase items for your home.

Rice – $250

Bread – $180

Milk – $220

Juice – $150

Task 1: Calculate the total cost.

Task 2: How much money will remain?

Task 3: If you add an item costing $120, will you stay within budget?

Suggested answers:

1. $250 + $180 + $220 + $150 = $800.

2. $1,000 − $800 = $200 remaining.

3. $800 + $120 = $920. Yes, you stay within budget. $1,000 − $920 = $80 remaining.

ACTIVITY 2

PLANNING A SCHOOL GARDEN

Scenario: Your class is helping to create a rectangular vegetable garden at school.

Length: 8 metres

Width: 5 metres

Number of planting rows: 4

Seedlings in each row: 6

Task 1: What is the length of the garden?

Task 2: What is the width of the garden?

Task 3: Calculate the perimeter.

Task 4: How many seedlings will be planted altogether?

Task 5: If the class buys 30 seedlings, how many will be left over?

Suggested answers:

1. Length = 8 metres.

2. Width = 5 metres.

3. Perimeter = 8 + 5 + 8 + 5 = 26 metres.

4. 4 × 6 = 24 seedlings.

5. 30 − 24 = 6 seedlings left over.

ACTIVITY 3

ORGANISING A CLASS PARTY

Scenario: Your class is organising a party for 24 students. The teacher has a budget of $5,000.

Juice – $1,200

Sandwiches – $2,400

Fruit – $800

Paper plates and cups – $350

Task 1: Calculate the total cost.

Task 2: How much money will remain?

Task 3: How many students will attend?

Task 4: Decorations cost $300. Is the remaining budget enough? Explain.

Suggested answers:

1. $1,200 + $2,400 + $800 + $350 = $4,750.

2. $5,000 − $4,750 = $250 remaining.

3. 24 students.

4. No. The decorations cost $300 but only $250 remains. The class is $50 short.

ACTIVITY 4

PLANNING A SCHOOL TRIP

Scenario: A Grade 4 class is planning a school trip. There are 36 students. Each bus can carry 12 students.

Bus cost: $3,000 per bus

Entry fee: $150 per student

Number of students: 36

Task 1: How many buses are needed?

Task 2: Calculate the total bus cost.

Task 3: Calculate the total entry fees.

Task 4: What is the total cost of the trip?

Task 5: If the school collects $10,000, how much more money is needed?

Suggested answers:

1. 36 ÷ 12 = 3 buses.

2. 3 × $3,000 = $9,000.

3. 36 × $150 = $5,400.

4. $9,000 + $5,400 = $14,400.

5. $14,400 − $10,000 = $4,400 more is needed.

ACTIVITY 5

MEASURING THE CLASSROOM

Scenario: Your class wants to put a border around the classroom floor. The floor is rectangular.

Length: 9 metres

Width: 6 metres

Each roll of border material covers 10 metres.

Task 1: Calculate the perimeter of the classroom.

Task 2: How many metres of border material are needed?

Task 3: How many rolls must be purchased?

Task 4: How many metres of material will be left over?

Suggested answers:

1. Perimeter = 9 + 6 + 9 + 6 = 30 metres.

2. 30 metres are needed.

3. 30 ÷ 10 = 3 rolls.

4. 3 rolls provide 30 metres. 0 metres left over.

ACTIVITY 6

RUNNING A MINI SHOP

Scenario: You are helping to operate a small shop during a school fair.

Notebooks: 5 at $120 each

Pencils: 3 at $40 each

Erasers: 2 at $50 each

A customer gives you $1,000.

Task 1: Calculate the cost of the notebooks.

Task 2: Calculate the cost of the pencils.

Task 3: Calculate the cost of the erasers.

Task 4: Find the total cost of all the items.

Task 5: How much change should the customer receive?

Suggested answers:

1. 5 × $120 = $600.

2. 3 × $40 = $120.

3. 2 × $50 = $100.

4. $600 + $120 + $100 = $820.

5. $1,000 − $820 = $180 change.

ACTIVITY 7

PREPARING A WEEKLY MEAL BUDGET

Scenario: A family sets aside $3,000 for food items during the school week.

Monday – $450

Tuesday – $350

Wednesday – $500

Thursday – $400

Friday – $550

Task 1: Calculate the total amount spent.

Task 2: How much money remains from the budget?

Task 3: Which day had the highest spending?

Task 4: What is the difference between the highest and lowest daily spending?

Suggested answers:

1. $450 + $350 + $500 + $400 + $550 = $2,250.

2. $3,000 − $2,250 = $750 remaining.

3. Friday, with $550.

4. $550 − $350 = $200 difference.

ACTIVITY 8

READING AND COMPARING DATA

Scenario: A class survey asked students to choose their favourite fruit.

Mango – 12 students

Orange – 8 students

Banana – 15 students

Watermelon – 5 students

Task 1: How many students took part in the survey?

Task 2: Which fruit was the most popular?

Task 3: Which fruit was the least popular?

Task 4: How many more students chose bananas than oranges?

Task 5: How many students chose mangoes and watermelons altogether?

Suggested answers:

1. 12 + 8 + 15 + 5 = 40 students.

2. Banana, chosen by 15 students.

3. Watermelon, chosen by 5 students.

4. 15 − 8 = 7 more students.

5. 12 + 5 = 17 students.

ACTIVITY 9

SHARING SUPPLIES EQUALLY

Scenario: A teacher receives supplies for four Grade 4 groups. The supplies must be shared equally.

48 pencils

32 exercise books

20 rulers

4 groups of students

Task 1: How many pencils will each group receive?

Task 2: How many exercise books will each group receive?

Task 3: How many rulers will each group receive?

Task 4: How many items will each group receive altogether?

Task 5: Explain how you know the supplies have been shared equally.

Suggested answers:

1. 48 ÷ 4 = 12 pencils.

2. 32 ÷ 4 = 8 exercise books.

3. 20 ÷ 4 = 5 rulers.

4. 12 + 8 + 5 = 25 items.

5. Each group receives the same number of each type of item. This means the supplies are shared equally.

ACTIVITY 10

DESIGNING A SCHOOL PLAYGROUND

Scenario: Your class is helping to plan a rectangular play area at school.

Length: 12 metres

Width: 7 metres

Fencing costs $200 per metre.

The school has a fencing budget of $8,000.

Task 1: Calculate the perimeter of the play area.

Task 2: How many metres of fencing are needed?

Task 3: Calculate the total cost of the fencing.

Task 4: Will the $8,000 budget be enough?

Task 5: How much money will remain or how much more will be needed?

Suggested answers:

1. Perimeter = 12 + 7 + 12 + 7 = 38 metres.

2. 38 metres of fencing are needed.

3. 38 × $200 = $7,600.

4. Yes, the budget is enough.

5. $8,000 − $7,600 = $400 remaining.
